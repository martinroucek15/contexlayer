# Customer Success Playbook

## Purpose

This playbook defines how Customer Success evaluates company health, manages enterprise onboarding, and prioritizes renewal risk. It is intended for internal demo workflows, executive reviews, and customer health analysis.

## Customer Success KPIs

| KPI | Definition | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly Revenue | Recognized subscription revenue for the customer in a given month. | Indicates account expansion, contraction, and customer segment mix. |
| Support Tickets | Number of support requests opened during the month. | Rising volume can signal product friction or onboarding issues. |
| Average Resolution Hours | Average time from ticket creation to resolution. | Longer resolution times usually reduce customer satisfaction. |
| NPS | Net Promoter Score from customer survey responses. | Leading indicator for renewal sentiment and advocacy. |
| Renewed | Whether the customer renewed in the period. | Final lagging indicator of customer health. |

## NPS Definitions

- **Promoters:** NPS score of 50 or higher.
- **Passives:** NPS score from 20 to 49.
- **Detractors:** NPS score below 20.

For executive reporting, focus on movement over time rather than a single month. A declining NPS trend alongside rising support tickets should be treated as a meaningful customer health warning.

## Support SLA

| Customer Plan | Target First Response | Target Resolution |
|---|---:|---:|
| Startup | 24 hours | 72 hours |
| Business | 12 hours | 48 hours |
| Enterprise | 4 hours | 24 hours |

Enterprise SLA breaches should be escalated to the account CSM and Support Engineering lead.

## Enterprise Onboarding Notes

Enterprise customers typically expand faster, generate more revenue, and open more support tickets during onboarding. Higher support volume is not automatically negative, but it becomes a risk when resolution time rises at the same time.

Watch for the following enterprise onboarding signals:

1. High ticket volume in the first three months.
2. Average resolution time above SLA target.
3. NPS decline after expansion.
4. Multiple support-heavy stakeholders in the same account.
5. Renewal decision maker not present in onboarding calls.

## Leadership Priorities

Leadership is currently focused on the following health narrative:

- Revenue is increasing.
- Enterprise customers are becoming a larger share of the customer base.
- Support ticket volume is increasing.
- Average resolution time is increasing.
- NPS is declining.

This does not mean the business is unhealthy. It means growth is creating operational pressure, especially in enterprise accounts. The recommended analysis should explain whether support capacity and enterprise onboarding are keeping pace with revenue expansion.

## Recommended Demo Question

> Revenue is growing, but are customers getting healthier or less healthy over time? Visualize the trend and explain what is happening.

## Expected Insight

The strongest answer should identify that monthly revenue and enterprise mix are rising while support burden and resolution time are also rising. NPS declines as support pressure increases, suggesting the company may need to invest in enterprise onboarding, support staffing, or product enablement before renewal risk increases further.
